# Bay Area Arts, Culture & Curiosities — week of 17 August 2026

Monday 17 August – Sunday 23 August. 126 listings, ordered by remove from
Mountain View.

## Week at a Glance

**One Berkeley room has three of the week's best bookings.** Freight & Salvage
takes Bobby McFerrin on Monday, Zola Jesus on Friday and Jeff Parker's ETA IVtet
on Saturday — a voice with ten Grammys, a gothic soprano, and the guitarist who
spent years inventing a patient, loop-like species of jazz in a Highland Park
bar. If you only pay attention to one venue this week, cross the bridge for that
one.

**The other spine is a four-night festival in a 350-capacity room.** SF Bay Pop
Fest takes over Bottom of the Hill from Thursday to Sunday, and Friday's bill —
The Aislers Set, Kids On a Crime Spree, Lightheaded, Wifey — is the thing to get
to. The Aislers Set made three records for Slumberland between 1998 and 2003 and
have surfaced perhaps a dozen times since. This is one of those times.

**Saturday and Friday are now near-tied for the crowded night** — 31 and 30
listings. Saturday's free layer is the better one: Oakland Chinatown StreetFest,
Rockridge Rock-N-Stroll and Afrobeats at the Lake all run in the East Bay within
four miles of each other; Duboce ParkJam, the World Arts West legacy panel and
free admission at both the de Young and the Legion of Honor run in the city. You
cannot do all of it, and the geography forces a choice: pick a side of the bay
by lunchtime.

**Monday is the quiet night, and it is not remotely quiet.** Seven listings, of
which three are Cat Power in a 500-seat Peninsula theatre, Bobby McFerrin in a
600-seat Berkeley listening room, and Valerie June at the Chapel. That is a
better Monday than most Saturdays get.

**Thursday is the connoisseur's night.** Scott Amendola, Fred Frith, Larry Ochs
and Ben Goldberg are playing a 150-capacity Albany bar for what a film ticket
costs. Nothing else this week has that ratio.

**The summer free-concert spine has ended.** Stern Grove's season closed on
16 August with Al Green; Oakland Municipal Band's Concerts at the Lake finished
on 2 August. Both appear in `sources.yml` as standing programming and neither
runs this window. What is left of the free layer is street fairs and the
Peninsula's Thursday/Friday series, which run into September.

**Nothing in the outer region clears the bar this week.** See the note at the
end.

## Monday 17 August

*Peninsula*

**Cat Power: The Greatest Tour** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm.
**Featured.** Chan Marshall revisiting the Memphis-soul record she cut in 2006:
smoky, unhurried, sung barely above a whisper. A 500-seat room is an absurdly
small place to see her, and the Peninsula almost never gets a booking of this
size. Caltrain Menlo Park is four minutes' walk and street parking is
two-hour-limited — take the train.

*San Francisco*

**Valerie June, Tianna Esperanza** — The Chapel, 8pm, $45–50. Tennessee
songwriter whose voice sits somewhere between Appalachian ballad and Memphis
gospel; banjo, dulcimer, and a register that goes thin and high on purpose. Two
nights here, and Monday's crowd is usually the quieter listen. Straight conflict
with both Cat Power and Bobby McFerrin — this is the fullest Monday of the year.

**Comedy 101: O Brother, Where Art Thou?** — Roxie, 6pm. The Coens' Depression-era
Odyssey. The sepia grade still looks better projected than streamed.

**American Psycho** — Roxie, 8.35pm. Harron's satire, which has aged into
something closer to documentary.

**Death Guild** — DNA Lounge, 18+. The oldest continuously running goth/industrial
night in the world, six DJs across two rooms. *Door time and price were not
posted — verify.*

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Bobby McFerrin** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. **Featured.** Ten Grammys, and
still the only person who can hold a 600-seat room with nothing but a voice and a
chest. One of three residency nights at the Freight this month. *Set time and
price were not on the listing that carried the date — check before you go.*

**Azure McCall** — Yoshi's, 8pm, $25–45. Bay Area standards singer doing the
annual birthday set. Straight-ahead, warm, and the cheapest thing on Yoshi's
calendar all week.

## Tuesday 18 August

*South Bay*

**Gipsy Kings featuring Nicolas Reyes** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga, 7.30pm,
from $67. Rumba catalana from the Camargue: nylon-string strumming, group
vocals, the songs everyone knows by the chorus. The hillside amphitheatre suits
them better than any indoor room would. $20 parking with a shuttle up the hill.

**Sips & Sounds of Summer** — Santana Row Park, San Jose, 6–8pm, free. *The act
is unannounced here — Santana Row's own page was down at compile time. The
series books local covers and light jazz.*

**IV and the Strange Band, Clownvis Presley** — The Ritz, San Jose, 7pm. Gothic
outlaw country from Hank Williams III's son, with a Presley-impersonating clown
opening. Exactly as advertised, which is the appeal.

*San Francisco*

**Valerie June** — The Chapel, 8pm. Second of two nights.

**Los Mirlos** — Regency Ballroom. The Amazonian chicha band: surf-guitar
tremolo laid over cumbia rhythm, invented in Peru in the early seventies and
still played by people who were there. One of the week's genuinely rare
bookings. *Door time not published.*

**Ida, Tara Jane O'Neil, Storey Littleton** — Bottom of the Hill, 8.30pm,
$20/$25. Ida's close-harmony slowcore was the quiet end of 90s indie and has
aged well. Tara Jane O'Neil opens with the same instinct for space. All ages,
and a bill that rewards standing still. **Best of the night in the city.**

**Civic Center Soundtrack** — Civic Center Plaza, 12–3pm, free. Lunchtime music
and food trucks. *Booking announced weekly; unknown at compile time.*

**My Night at Maud's** — Roxie, 6pm. Rohmer's third Moral Tale: two hours of
Catholics arguing about Pascal and desire, and somehow the most romantic thing
playing this week.

**Train to Busan, 10th anniversary** — Roxie, 8.35pm. Still plays best with a
full, loud room.

**Nerd Nite SF #163** — Rickshaw Stop, 8pm, $10–15. Three people give slide talks
on whatever they are obsessed with, in a rock club, to a drinking audience.
Running since 2003 and the purest expression of the oddities beat there is.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**The Stylistics** — Yoshi's, 8pm, $69–109. Philadelphia soul in its sweetest
register: Thom Bell arrangements, falsetto leads. Three nights, and the room is
the right size for it.

## Wednesday 19 August

*South Bay*

**Men At Work** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga, 7pm, from $70. Colin Hay fronting
the Australian new-wave catalogue. *A ticketing feed lists Toad the Wet Sprocket
and Shonen Knife as support — an odd enough bill that it needs checking; the
venue's own calendar would not load.*

**Ne-Yo and Akon: Nights Like This** — Shoreline Amphitheatre, Mountain View,
8pm, from $68. Two mid-2000s radio catalogues on a co-headline bill. Findable
without help, but it is the only thing at Shoreline midweek and the lawn is
cheap. $40 to park; the bike path from Mountain View is the better answer.

**Omar Courtz** — SAP Center, San Jose, 8pm, from $42. Puerto Rican urbano.
Listed for completeness — an arena is the wrong room for what he does.

*San Francisco*

**Silkworm, Nina Nastasia** — Bottom of the Hill, 8.30pm, $25/$30, 21+.
**Featured, and the best thing in the region on a Wednesday.** Silkworm played
taut, funny, unsentimental rock for fifteen years and stopped in 2005 when
Michael Dahlquist was killed; they play rarely and never casually. Nina Nastasia
opens with the bleakest and best songwriting on any bill this week.

**Angine de Poitrine, Population II** — The Independent, 8pm. Montréal psych,
knotty and drum-led. **Sold out** — for ticket-holders only.

**Pi'erre Bourne** — The Fillmore. Atlanta producer-rapper whose beats defined a
chunk of late-2010s rap before he stepped to the mic himself. The chandeliers
and the free apples are the other half of the ticket. *Door time not published.*

**Free Wednesday at YBCA** — Yerba Buena Center for the Arts. Galleries free
every Wednesday; GaHee Park's *Behind the Curtain* is prettier than it is
comfortable. A panel, Creative Power Moves, runs the same day.

**Hairspray** — San Francisco Playhouse, 2pm matinee, from $47. Running to 12
September, with the Playhouse's second stage, *R.O.I.*, playing the same weeks.

**Creative Resource Library: Synth Sessions** — Gray Area, free. Open-bench
evening with hands on the modular gear rather than a performance. *Time not
published.*

**The Sword, Red Fang, The Spoon Benders** — Regency Ballroom. Two of the better
stoner-metal bands of the last twenty years on one bill — the Sword's
Sabbath-shaped riffs against Red Fang's blunter, funnier take. *Door time not
published.*

**Deb Never** — Rickshaw Stop, 8pm, $20–25. Grunge-inflected bedroom R&B, sung
flat and low over guitars that are louder than the genre usually allows.

**Drunken Noodles** — Roxie, 8pm. Lucio Castro's follow-up to End of the Century.
The Roxie is the only Bay Area house playing it.

*Peninsula*

**Filoli Gardens Summer Nights** — Filoli, Woodside, from $10. The formal gardens
open into the evening; opening night of a run to 3 September. *Which nights of
the week it runs was not stated on the only fetchable listing — confirm before
driving to Woodside.*

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Exhibition tour: Maren Hassinger, Living Moving Growing** — BAMPFA, 12.15pm,
free with admission. Fifty years of making wire rope behave like foliage. The
lunchtime slot is the least crowded way to see the show.

**The Stylistics** — Yoshi's, 8pm. Night two of three.

## Thursday 20 August

*South Bay*

**The Black Crowes, Whiskey Myers, Southall** — Shoreline Amphitheatre,
Mountain View, 6.30pm, from $32. The Robinson brothers are speaking again and
the band sounds like 1992 on a good night. Long bill — doors are early and
Southall goes on before most people have parked.

*Peninsula*

**Summer Concert Series: Native Elements** — Colma Community Center, 6–8pm,
free. Bay Area roots-reggae on the grass. BART Colma is a ten-minute walk, and
this is one of the few Peninsula shows where transit genuinely beats driving.

**Movies on the Square: F1** — Courthouse Square, Redwood City, family film at
6, main picture 8.30pm, free. Caltrain Redwood City is three minutes away, which
matters because the square's garage fills by 7.

**Beats, Brews & Vines: Pride and Joy** — Downtown Millbrae, 6–8pm, free.
Third-Thursday street party at Broadway and Hillcrest. Motown and Stax covers
with a horn section — better than the format usually gets.

*Three free Peninsula concerts on one night, all within twenty miles.* Colma is
the best music, Redwood City the best setting, Millbrae the shortest hop off
101. They overlap almost exactly, so pick one.

*San Francisco*

**SF Bay Pop Fest night 1: Tony Molina, Galore, Paper Jam, Monster Treasure** —
Bottom of the Hill, 8pm, $30. Tony Molina writes ninety-second songs with Thin
Lizzy guitar leads and Guided by Voices brevity; Monster Treasure are the
loudest thing on the pass.

**Portraits of Past, The Saddest Landscape, Drought** — Great American Music
Hall, 8pm, $23–25. East Bay screamo from the mid-90s, playing the record that
half of the genre's second wave was built on. The balcony is a strange and
excellent place to watch a band this loud.

**G Flip, Vienna Vienna** — The Independent, 8pm. Australian drummer-songwriter
who plays kit standing up and sings over it — a genuinely physical pop show.

**BAILEN, BEL.** — Café du Nord, 8pm. Three siblings doing tight, close-harmony
indie folk — the blend is the instrument, and it only really works live.

**Beach Goth Night** — Rickshaw Stop, 9pm, $10–15. DJ night built on the
surf-and-eyeliner end of the 2010s, cheap and late.

**Downtown SF free outdoor EDM party** — 63 Ellis Street, 5–10pm, free. Ellis
between Powell and Stockton closed and turned into an open-air stage. *DJs not
published.*

**Third Thursdays on Treat: Treat's Got Talent** — Treat Cut, 5–9pm, free.
Neighbourhood talent show on a closed Mission block. Small, genuinely local,
first year of the talent format.

**CatVideoFest 2026** — Roxie, 6pm. Seventy minutes of curated cat footage,
proceeds to local shelters. Exactly what it says, and the room is always full of
people who came on purpose.

**Luchino Visconti's White Nights, 4K restoration** — Roxie, 6pm. Dostoevsky in
a fog-bound studio-built Livorno. The restoration is the reason to go: it was
shot to look artificial and now it finally does.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Scott Amendola with Fred Frith, Larry Ochs, Ben Goldberg** — Ivy Room, Albany.
**Featured, and the best-value ticket of the week by a wide margin.** Four of the
most serious improvisers in Northern California in a 150-capacity bar; Frith
alone has been redefining what a guitar is since Henry Cow. *Door time not
listed.*

**The Stylistics** — Yoshi's, 8pm. Final night.

Third-Friday mass ride; route announced the week of. Somewhere between a parade
and a rolling block party.

**San Jose Cactus & Succulent Fair, evening sale** — Peterson Middle School,
Sunnyvale, 6–9pm, free. Friday evening is the serious-collector session — the
rare stuff goes in the first hour.

**Lupillo Rivera, El As De La Sierra, Rogelio Martínez, Leonel El Ranchero** —
San Jose Civic. Banda and norteño on a four-deep bill; Rivera has been a fixture

*Peninsula*

**Music on the Square: Foreverland** — Courthouse Square, Redwood City, 6–8pm,
free. Fourteen-piece Michael Jackson band: no wig, no glove, just the

**Inner Wave, Pillowprince** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm. LA five-piece

*San Francisco*

Kids On a Crime Spree share half a lineage and most of the same guitar sound.

**Jailbreak at 50: The Rockers, Slough Feg, Buncrana** — Great American Music

have been playing twin-guitar heroic metal in this city for thirty years and do
not need the excuse. Tribute framing, real band underneath.

**Dimmu Borgir, Hulder** — The Warfield, 7.30pm. Norwegian symphonic black metal
at full orchestral bombast. Hulder opens with something rawer and colder, and is
the better band on the bill.

**Niina Soleil, Rex & The Rhythm, Camille Trust** — The Independent, 9pm. Three
deep on contemporary soul and funk revivalism. *Descriptor is lower-confidence —
none of the three has much recorded output to check against.*

**Cocteaufest SF night 1** — Cat Club, Folsom Street. Twenty-third year of a
Cocteau Twins fan gathering that has outlasted most actual bands. *Lineup and
door not posted; Saturday at the Chapel is the anchor.*

**Electric Duke (Bowie), Temptation (New Order)** — The Chapel, 9pm, $20–25. Two
tribute acts. A real part of the circuit, listed accordingly — but there are
four original bands playing within a mile of here tonight.

**DWLLRS, thebandfriday** — Café du Nord, 8pm. Sun-bleached duo making the kind
of loose, jazz-chorded pop that soundtracks other people's summers.

**LCD Soundsystem vs Daft Punk Night** — Rickshaw Stop, 8pm, $15–20. A DJ night
pitting two catalogues against each other. No live band, and the listing says so

**Nine Vicious** — The Fillmore. A booking with little recorded trail; the room
is the draw more than the name. *Descriptor is lower-confidence, door time not
published.*

**Pierce Warnecke and Matthew Biederman: Phytomorphic Topologies** — Gray Area.
Audiovisual work built on plant-derived forms, in a former cinema kitted out for
exactly this. Closer to installation than gig. *Time and price not published.*

**Oliver Koletzki, Course Correction** — Public Works. Melodic, faintly
folk-inflected house from the Stil vor Talent label boss, with a separate bill
in the Loft. Flagged a PW Picks night, so the venue's membership programme gets
in free.

**Mustache Harbor: Yacht Rock Forever** — Bimbo's 365 Club, 8pm, 21+. Soft-rock
covers in a 1931 supper club with a mirrored backdrop, which is genuinely the
right room for it.

**Block Rockin' Beats: 90s Rave** — DNA Lounge, 21+. Big beat, acid house and
trance played straight, alongside a 90s tribute-band night in the other room.
*No time or price posted.*

**SF Queer Film Fest: opening night** — Roxie, 5.30pm. Sixth year, three days,
deliberately programmed around filmmakers Frameline's scale tends to miss.
Opening night usually sells through first.

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Friday Nights at OMCA: Sabrina Shauna** — Oakland Museum of California, 5–9pm.
Off the Grid trucks in the garden, live set, galleries open late — Mildred
Howard's *Poetics of Memory* is the one to see. Outdoor block free, galleries at

**Coyote Island, Mount Saint Elias** — Cornerstone, Berkeley. **Sold out.** Beer

folk-adjacent.

**Zola Jesus** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. Nika Roza Danilova sings gothic
electronic music with an operatically trained voice, and the Freight's dead-quiet
room is a strange, good place for it. *Time and price not listed on the source
that carried the date.*

**Norman Brown** — Yoshi's, 8pm, $49–94. Smooth-jazz guitar of the Benson
lineage, played cleanly and without irony. Two nights.

**Oakland Ballers vs Modesto Roadsters** — Raimondi Park. First of three at home.
*The schedule source gave an implausible first pitch for this date, so the time
is unconfirmed — the Friday game is usually early evening.*

## Saturday 22 August

The crowded day. 31 listings; the free ones are as good as the ticketed ones.

*South Bay*

**San Jose Cactus & Succulent Fair** — Peterson Middle School, Sunnyvale,
8am–6pm, free. Forty-plus vendors, competitive horticulture as spectator sport.
The show tables are worth as much time as the sales floor.

**Wobbleland 2026, day 2** — San Jose McEnery Convention Center.

*Peninsula*

**The Untouchables, The Inciters** — The Guild Theatre, Menlo Park, 8pm. LA
mod-ska survivors from the early 80s, with Oakland's Inciters bringing a
nine-piece soul revue underneath. A dancing show in a seated room, which usually
resolves in the dancing's favour.

**Bay Area Chocolate Chip Cookie Festival** — Museum of American Heritage, Palo
Alto, 1–4pm, free. A judged cookie competition on the lawn of a small technology
museum. Exactly as narrow as it sounds, which is the appeal.

*San Francisco*

**The Breeders, Imperial Teen** — Castro Theatre, 8pm. **Featured.** The Deal
sisters in a 1922 movie palace, with Imperial Teen opening — as close to a
perfect bill as this week produces. **Both nights sold out**; the only route in
now is a resale, so plan around it rather than for it.

**Cocteaufest SF night 2: Simon Raymonde (DJ set), Sugar Hiccup, Mocteau Twins**
— The Chapel, 8pm, $20–25. First time in twenty-three years of Cocteaufest that
an actual Cocteau Twin turns up. Raymonde DJs from 8 and between the bands; the
tribute acts are beside the point, Raymonde in a church-shaped room is not.

**SF Bay Pop Fest night 3: Dear Nora, Jeanines, The Kitchenettes, Pillow Fight**
— Bottom of the Hill, 8pm, $30. Katy Davidson's Dear Nora writes short, plain,
devastating songs and has been quietly influential for twenty-five years.
Jeanines make two-minute jangle records that sound cut in a 1983 basement.

**Non Stop Bhangra: Crash an Indian Wedding Party** — Great American Music Hall,
7pm, $25–30. Twenty years of dhol, Punjabi folk and club bass in the same room,
with a dance lesson up front so nobody has an excuse. Early door — 6pm.

**Pearl & The Oysters, Chris Cohen (solo)** — The Independent, 9pm.
French-by-way-of-Florida duo making weightless, Stereolab-adjacent lounge pop.
Chris Cohen opens solo, the quieter and stranger half of the evening.

**The Charities, Sweet Lew** — Café du Nord, 8pm. Straight-ahead guitar band on a
Live Nation-promoted bill; the room is better than the promotion suggests.

**Little Stranger, Tropidelic, Jarv, Damn Skippy** — The Fillmore. Party-rap and
funk four deep, on a bill built for a room that dances rather than watches.
*Door time not published.*

**SF Electronic Music Festival: 25th anniversary gala** — Gray Area. Twenty-five
years of the city's longest-running experimental electronic festival, marked in
the room that has hosted much of it. *Lineup, time and price were not on the
calendar listing — worth chasing, because an anniversary bill is usually the
strongest of its year.*

**Generaiderz: LeNtO San Francisco — MURDER CLUB, exmantera, Gabberbitch69** —
Public Works. Fast, abrasive club music across both rooms: gabber and hard drum
rather than house. *Door time and price not published.*

**Emo Nite** — Rickshaw Stop, 9pm, $20–22. The touring emo DJ night. Standing
format, listed for completeness.

**Duboce ParkJam** — Duboce Park, 12–5pm, free. Five local acts, an hour each, on
the grass. The N Judah stops at the park's edge. Bring a layer — Duboce catches
the fog earlier than the Mission does.

**World Arts West: legacy artist panel** — SF Main Library, Koret Auditorium,
2–4pm, free. Deborah Vaughan, Mythili Kumar, Naomi Diouf and Roy & PJ Hirabayashi
in conversation; between them, most of the institutional memory of Bay Area world
dance. Doors 1.30, reception after.

**Free Saturday: de Young Museum** and **Legion of Honor** — both 9.30am–5.15pm,
general admission waived, special exhibitions excepted. They pair into a whole
Saturday if you want one.

**The Sound 2026** — Golden Gate Park, 12–4pm, free. Open-air music and arts
festival. *Lineup not published.*

**Dust Breaks: Shakti EP release** — DNA Lounge, 18+. Drum & bass, breakbeat and
dubstep for a record release. *No door time or price posted.*

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Jeff Parker and ETA IVtet** — Freight & Salvage, Berkeley. **Featured, and the
best ticket of the weekend.** Tortoise's guitarist leading the quartet that spent
years in a tiny Highland Park bar inventing a patient, loop-like species of jazz.
On record it is one of the decade's best; live it is longer and stranger. *Set
time not listed.*

**Oakland Chinatown StreetFest** — Oakland Chinatown, 10am–5pm, free. Two days of
lion dance, food stalls, local makers and a ping-pong tournament across closed
streets. The largest free thing in the region this
weekend and the one most worth crossing the bridge for. **Take BART** — 12th
Street is a block away and Chinatown parking is hopeless during it.

**Afrobeats at the Lake: Juls, DJ Neptune** — Snow Park, Lake Merritt, 1–8pm,
ticketed. Eighth year, two stages, forty vendors, seven hours.
Juls produced for Burna Boy and Wizkid and is a better DJ than either's records
suggest; DJ Neptune has been shaping Nigerian radio for twenty years. Under-16s
free. Metered parking only — BART or ride-share.

**Rockridge Rock-N-Stroll** — College Avenue, 11am–4pm, free. Twenty-plus bands
and fifty local makers along a mile of College Avenue. Sidewalks and patios
rather than a closed street, so the traffic stays and the scale stays human.
Rockridge BART drops you in the middle.

*Three good free East Bay events, all Saturday daytime, all within four miles.*
Chinatown StreetFest and Rock-N-Stroll are both 11–4 and genuinely conflict;
Afrobeats runs to 8 and is the one that survives doing something else first.

**SF Mime Troupe: WRECKAGE** — Willard Park, Berkeley, 2pm, free. Sixty-seventh
season of free agitprop musicals in public parks, and still the only company
doing it. Live music half an hour before; bring something to sit on.

**Pom Poko** — BAMPFA, 3.30pm. Takahata's film about shape-shifting raccoon dogs
losing a war against suburban development, which is funnier and sadder than that
sounds.

**Montclair free outdoor cinema** — Montclair Village, 7pm, free. *Title not
announced.*

**Mismiths, Debased, Ceremony81** — Ivy Room, Albany. Misfits covers at the top
of a three-band punk bill in a small room. *Door time not listed.*

**Norman Brown** — Yoshi's, 8pm. Second of two nights.

**Oakland Ballers vs Modesto Roadsters** — Raimondi Park, 7.35pm.
Independent-league ball in a 4,000-seat park in West Oakland, and the cheapest
night out in this digest. BART West Oakland is a fifteen-minute walk.

*South Bay*

**Allen Stone, The Dip** — Mountain Winery, Saratoga, 7.30pm. Blue-eyed soul

afternoon, kirtan and stalls at the meadow end. One of the oldest recurring
public festivals in the city.

**Sunday Streets: Bayview** — Third Street, 12–4pm, free. A mile of Third Street
closed to cars and handed to the neighbourhood. The least touristed edition of
the season and the most locally programmed. The Muni T runs the length of it.

**Golden Gate Park Band: a tribute to Ukraine** — Spreckels Temple of Music,
1–2.30pm, free. The city's municipal band has played free Sunday concerts at
this bandshell since 1882, and this is its 144th season. Ninety minutes, no
intermission, no ticket. Bring a layer — the Music Concourse fogs early.

**Lindy in the Park** — JFK Drive, Golden Gate Park, 11am–2pm, free. Free swing
dancing on the closed stretch of JFK since 1996. Beginner lesson at 12, no
partner needed, nobody minds if you just watch.

**Suds Francisco: funk and fermentation** — Salesforce Park, 2–7pm, $68. Live
funk and small-producer beer, cider and kombucha on the elevated park above the
transit centre. Not cheap, but a garden on a roof over a bus deck is the reason
to go.

**Hippie Death Cult** — Rickshaw Stop, 8pm, $18–23. Portland doom with an actual
singer rather than a growler — heavy, slow, and more tuneful than the name
promises.

**SF Queer Film Fest: closing night** — Roxie. Includes the world premiere of
*In This Moment*. *Start time was not fixed on either page at compile time.*

*Berkeley & Oakland*

**Keyon Harrold** — Yoshi's, 7pm, $33–69. **Featured.** The trumpet on Don
Cheadle's *Miles Ahead* and a long list of hip-hop records, leading his own band
in a room that suits him. The most serious jazz booking of the week, and the
only one on this list where the horn is doing the writing.

**Oakland Chinatown StreetFest, day 2** — 10am–5pm, free. Usually the calmer of
the two days, and better if you actually want to eat and talk to people rather
than queue.

**My Neighbor Totoro** (2pm) and **Grave of the Fireflies** (4.30pm, sold out) —
BAMPFA. Programmed back to back exactly as the original 1988 double bill was, a
scheduling decision that was cruel then and remains so.

**SF Mime Troupe: WRECKAGE** — Willard Park, Berkeley, 2pm, free. Final Berkeley
date of the run.

**Oakland Ballers vs Modesto Roadsters** — Raimondi Park, 4.05pm. Sunday matinee
to close the homestand. Afternoon ball at Raimondi is the best-value thing in the
East Bay this week.

**Montclair Picnic on the Plaza** — Montclair Village, 12–4pm, free. Small
hills-neighbourhood afternoon. Modest by design and pleasant for it.
