*Compiled Wednesday, August 12, 2026 · Organized by day, then by distance from Mountain View*

**South Bay** (San Jose, Saratoga, Sunnyvale, Mountain View) → **Peninsula** (Los Altos, Palo Alto, Menlo Park, Woodside, Redwood City, San Mateo, Colma) → **San Francisco** → **Berkeley & Oakland**

### The Week at a Glance

- **Saturday Aug 15 is the heaviest day** and it starts in your backyard — Toto at Shoreline, then a chain of options running all the way up to the Greek.
- **Stern Grove closes its season** with Public Enemy (Sat) and Al Green (Sun). Free, lottery-ticketed, and the last weekend.
- **Oakland Pride (Sun)** is the East Bay's center of gravity — parade and festival at Ogawa Plaza.
- **The Peninsula is unusually well-served** this weekend: Fleetwood Mask at Filoli twice, David Lee Roth in a 500-cap Menlo Park room, Head West in San Mateo.
- **Thursday and Friday are street-festival nights** in SF — Valencia LIVE! Thursday, Chinatown Night Market Friday.

## Peninsula

- **Fleetwood Mask** — Filoli Summer Stage, 86 Cañada Rd, Woodside
  - *Fleetwood Mac tribute — the full 70s catalog, performed on an outdoor stage in the estate meadow*

- **Music on the Square** — Courthouse Square, Downtown Redwood City, 6–8pm
  - *Weekly free series — the 2026 season spans funk, soul, Latin rock, and tribute bands*

## San Francisco

- **Chinatown Night Market** — Grant Ave between California and Pacific, 5–9pm
  - Voted the top night market in the country two years running; food vendors, shopping, live entertainment
  - *Music:* Traditional Chinese performance alongside contemporary Asian American acts
  - Free
- **Bay Area International Deaf Dance Festival** — Dance Mission Theater
  - Deaf and hard-of-hearing choreographers and companies
  - *Why it's interesting:* Choreography built for visual rhythm rather than audible beat — a different formal logic than most dance you'll see.
- **Tomahawk and Melvins** — The Warfield
  - *Experimental metal and sludge — Mike Patton's art-metal project sharing a bill with the band that essentially invented this whole lineage of heaviness. A serious noise pairing.*
- **Frak** with Del the Funky Homosapien, Guapdad 4000, G-Eazy, Nef the Pharoah, Mistah F.A.B. and more — The Independent
  - *Bay Area hip-hop showcase — an unusually deep local lineup spanning three generations of the scene, from Hieroglyphics-era veterans to current Vallejo and Oakland acts. Best hip-hop bill of the weekend.*
- **Man Man** with American Tomahawk — The Chapel
  - *Experimental rock and gypsy-punk — carnivalesque instrumentation, unhinged vocals, junkyard percussion*
- **What So Not** — The Midway (outdoor)
  - *Electronic and future bass — Australian producer, melodic drops and heavy sound design*
- **The Gallery, Carrellee, Skotos** — The Great Northern
  - *House and techno — proper club programming*
- **PLOSIVS and Tijuana Panthers** with Minus Numbers and Otoliths — Bottom of the Hill
  - *Post-punk and surf-garage — PLOSIVS features members of Drive Like Jehu and Pinback*

## Berkeley & Oakland

# SATURDAY, AUGUST 15

*The heaviest day of the window, and it opens close to home.*

## South Bay

- **Toto, Christopher Cross, The Romantics** — Shoreline Amphitheatre, Mountain View (outdoor)
  - *Yacht rock and classic pop-rock — Toto's session-musician precision, Christopher Cross's soft-rock catalog, The Romantics' power-pop. A full-throttle nostalgia bill, and it's five minutes from you.*
- **The Movement and Pepper** with Cydeways — Mountain Winery, Saratoga
  - *Reggae-rock and dub-pop — hillside outdoor setting, roughly 25 minutes out*
- **San Jose Giants** — Excite Ballpark

## Peninsula

- **Head West Marketplace** — South B Street, Downtown San Mateo, 11am–5pm
  - Curated local artists, vintage dealers, designers, and makers
  - *Music:* Live local sets alongside the market — acoustic and low-key
  - Free

## San Francisco

- **Stern Grove Festival: Public Enemy** with DJ Davey D — Sigmund Stern Grove, 19th Ave & Sloat
  - *Political hip-hop — the most consequential protest rap group in the genre's history, with the Bay's own Davey D DJing. Extraordinary booking for a free show.*
  - Free but lottery-ticketed; unclaimed tickets released, plus ~1,500 distributed in person via the Community Box Office the day before
  - *Practical:* Eucalyptus-shaded and reliably foggy. Layers, blanket, picnic. Season's biggest crowds — arrive early.
- **Dahlia Society of California Annual Show** — Hall of Flowers, Golden Gate Park
  - Growers from across the state bring their best blooms; judged in all categories including Best in Show
  - Draws thousands. Free.
  - *Why it's interesting:* The Hall of Flowers at full capacity is a genuinely overwhelming sensory experience, and the competitive-horticulture subculture on display is its own reward.
- **Pizza, Bagel, and Beer Festival** — North Beach
  - Exactly what it says. Walkable between vendors.
- **Quicksand, Bane, Soul Blind** — Castro Theatre
  - *Post-hardcore — Quicksand are foundational to the genre's melodic, heavy-but-textured wing; Bane bring straight-edge hardcore. Unusual to see this in a movie palace.*
- **Merola Grand Finale** — War Memorial Opera House
  - *Opera — arias, ensembles, and scenes from across the repertoire, showcasing the program's emerging artists. Optional post-show reception with the singers.*
- **CORTIS** — Bill Graham Civic Auditorium
  - *K-pop — first of two nights*
- **Gallant and Stefan Mahendra** — August Hall
  - *Alt-R&B — falsetto-driven, atmospheric production*

## Berkeley & Oakland

- **Thee Sacred Souls, LA LOM, The Womack Sisters** — Greek Theatre, UC Berkeley (outdoor)
  - *Retro soul and Chicano soul — Thee Sacred Souls play sweet, unhurried Daptone-style soul; LA LOM bring instrumental cumbia and boleros; The Womack Sisters carry a legendary soul family name. The best-curated outdoor bill of the weekend.*
- **311, Dirty Heads, Ocean Alley** — Toyota Pavilion at Concord (outdoor)
  - *Reggae-rock and alt-rock — 311's funk-metal-reggae fusion with two younger acts in the same lane. Big, sunny, uncomplicated.*
- **J. Holiday** — Yoshi's, Oakland
  - *Contemporary R&B — slow-jam register*

## Worth the Drive *(outside the four core geos)*

Marin, the North Bay, and Santa Cruz are out of regular coverage, but a few things this weekend clear the bar.

**Santa Cruz** — roughly 45 min from Mountain View over Highway 17

- **Atmosphere** with The Pharcyde, DJ Abilities, Abstract Rude — Quarry Amphitheater, Fri Aug 14
  - *Underground hip-hop — Rhymesayers-era Midwest rap alongside West Coast alternative hip-hop pioneers. The Pharcyde's presence makes this a genuine legacy bill, and the Quarry is a striking open-air venue carved into the UCSC campus. Arguably the strongest hip-hop lineup of the weekend outside The Independent.*
- **Ani DiFranco** with Valerie June — Santa Cruz Civic Auditorium, Sat Aug 15
  - *Folk-punk and singer-songwriter — DiFranco's percussive open-tuned guitar and political directness; Valerie June opens with an Appalachian-soul hybrid that's worth arriving early for. Two distinctive voices, well matched.*
- **The Polish Ambassador** — Felton Music Hall, Fri Aug 14
  - *Electronic and downtempo — funk-inflected, environmentally themed project; small room in the redwoods*
- **Davy Knowles** — Moe's Alley, Wed Aug 12 (carries into the window)
  - *Blues rock — Isle of Man guitarist working in a traditional blues vocabulary*

**Marin & North Bay** — 60–75 min from Mountain View, and you're crossing two bridges or the whole city

- **Etana** — Sweetwater Music Hall, Mill Valley, Thu Aug 13
  - *Reggae — Jamaican vocalist, roots-conscious songwriting with a soul edge*
- **Penny and Sparrow** with Cece Coakley — HopMonk Tavern, Novato, Fri Aug 14
  - *Indie folk — close male harmony, literary lyrics, spare arrangements*
- **Roller Disco Daze** — Lagoon Island, Marin County Fairgrounds, **final weekend, through Aug 16**
  - Free community roller rink: themed nights, lawn games, art installations
  - *Music:* DJ-driven disco, funk, roller-skate classics

*Honest assessment:* Santa Cruz is a real option — Highway 17 is a known quantity and Atmosphere is the single best argument for leaving the corridor this weekend. Marin is a stretch from Mountain View for anything on this list; it only makes sense if you're already north for another reason.

## Verify Before You Go

Lineups shift, shows move, and free-but-ticketed events have their own release mechanics. Stern Grove's closing weekend in particular will be heavily oversubscribed — check sterngrove.org for lottery and Community Box Office details well before Saturday.

Genre descriptions are editorial shorthand, not official billing. Where a venue's lineup was unannounced, the note describes the room's typical booking rather than the specific night.
